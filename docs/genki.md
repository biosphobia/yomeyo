# Genki I, analysed, and how the Genki tab teaches it

This is the planning document behind the **Genki** tab: what *Genki I: An
Integrated Course in Elementary Japanese* (3rd edition, 2020) contains, where
it is hard for someone studying without a teacher, and how the companion
course fills those gaps. Everything below is a summary in our own words; the
book itself is not reproduced anywhere in the app. Page numbers are book
pages.

## 1. The book at a glance

- **Who it's for.** Written at Kansai Gaidai for university courses. It works
  for self-study, but it assumes a teacher. Finishing Genki I puts you at
  roughly JLPT N5 / CEFR A1.
- **Two halves that run in parallel.**
  - **Conversation & Grammar (会話・文法編), pp. 30–294.** Greetings and
    numbers come first, then Lessons 1–12.
  - **Reading & Writing (読み書き編), pp. 296–356.** Its Lessons 1–2 teach
    hiragana and katakana. Lessons 3–12 teach 145 kanji (#001–145), each
    with a reading passage and a writing task. R&W lesson *n* assumes
    Conversation lesson *n* is already done.
- **Appendix, pp. 358–382.**
  - A grammar index.
  - Japanese→English and English→Japanese vocabulary indexes, with verb
    classes marked.
  - A map of Japan.
  - A sound-change chart for numbers and counters.
  - A verb conjugation chart. It covers verbs only, with no adjectives.
- **Every conversation lesson has the same shape:**
  - goals
  - dialogues, with audio
  - vocabulary, 50–60 words, with dialogue words starred and optional
    "additional vocabulary"
  - grammar notes
  - expression notes
  - practice
  - culture notes, Useful Expressions and a "Let's Find Out" research task
- **Script.** Lessons 1–2 use kana with romaji. From Lesson 3 the book uses
  kanji with furigana and drops romaji, so kana needs to be solid by the
  end of Lesson 2. The Kana tab already covers this.

| Lesson | Theme | Grammar | Main difficulty for a self-studier |
|---|---|---|---|

| 1 あたらしいともだち | introductions | X は Y です · か questions · の | Dropping the subject; は is read "wa"; の order is the reverse of English "of"; よじ/しちじ/くじ |
| 2 かいもの | shopping, ordering | これ/この/ここ series · だれの · も · じゃないです · ね/よ | The こそあど perspective flip; price sound changes (さんびゃく, はっせん…); counting in 万 |
| 3 デートのやくそく | daily routine, invitations | ます verbs and verb classes · を/で/に/へ · when に goes with time words · ませんか · frequency words · word order · topic は | u- vs ru-verbs (帰る); に vs で; which time words take に; ぜんぜん/あまり need a negative verb |
| 4 はじめてのデート | locations, the past | あります/います · location words · past です and past verbs · も with particles · durations · たくさん · と | Place に for where things are vs place で for actions; も replaces は/が/を but attaches to other particles; ごろ vs ぐらい |
| 5 おきなわりょこう | travel, describing | い/な adjectives (present, past, before nouns) · すき/きらい with が · ましょう · counters | ×さむいじゃないです; きれい and きらい are な-adjectives; いい→よくない |

| 7 かぞくのしゃしん | family, appearance | ている (in progress vs result) · Xは Yが adjective · adjective て-forms · stem + に行く · counting people | Telling apart the meanings of ている (結婚している, 知っている→知りません); verbs for putting on clothes |

| 10 ふゆやすみのよてい | plans, comparing | より/のほうが · いちばん · の for "one" · つもり · なる · どこかに/どこにも · で for means | どちら for two things vs どれ for three or more; ×暖かいになる |

Reading & Writing adds the kana (L1–2) and 14–16 kanji per lesson from L3

- a daily routine

- the Tanabata story

## 3. Where the book leaves a self-studier alone

1. **Classroom-only practice.** Roughly 35–50% of every practice section is
   pair work, group work or two-sided information gaps (for example L2
   pp. 79–80, L4 p. 126, L8, L9). Alone, you can do the solo drills and that
   is all.
2. **Explanations are short.** The "why" is left for the teacher to say:
   - why は and not が
   - why それ turns into これ in your answer
   - when に attaches to a time word
   - what ている means in a given sentence

   Key caveats sit in footnotes (L3 time に, L4 ごろ/ぐらい, L7 the verb
   classes for ている, L8 だ before と思う).
3. **No answer key, nobody to correct you.** Writing tasks and open
   questions get no feedback.
4. **Reference with no drills.** The appendix counter chart, the date
   readings (L4) and the minutes table (L1) have little or no practice
   attached.
5. **Points that build on each other.** Each of these needs regular review,
   not one pass:
   - verb classes (L3) feed the て-form (L6)
   - the て-form feeds ている (L7)
   - ている, with the L3 verb classes, feeds the short forms (L8–9)
   - the short forms feed everything after them (L10–12)
6. **Choosing the right register** (polite or casual, and who to use each
   with) is hard to pick up without hearing a teacher.

## 4. How the companion teaches it

Each lesson in the Genki tab follows the book's order and cites its pages, so
it sits beside a university syllabus without friction. Inside a lesson:

- **Overview.**
  - goals in plain words
  - the teacher's framing of the lesson
  - a five-day plan
  - a contents list with ticks
- **Vocabulary.**
  - Tap any word to hear it.
  - ★ marks the words from the lesson's dialogue, to learn first.
  - "Make it yours" groups hold personal words: your country, your major,
    your family.
  - A match game for each lesson.
- **One page per grammar point or topic**, with these parts:
  - a teacher-style explanation of *why*, not only *what*
  - a pattern box
  - spoken examples, with optional romaji
  - **Watch out**: the mistakes students actually make
  - **In class**: what quizzes and oral exams tend to test
  - **In real life**: how it sounds outside class
  - a **quick check**, where every answer comes with its reason
  - one or two optional **games**
- **Warm-ups** for greetings, numbers, big numbers/prices and classroom
  phrases. These are optional and never block progress.
- **Put it together.**
  - an original conversation using the lesson's grammar
  - tap a line for its English, or play the whole thing with pauses for
    shadowing
  - Lesson 1 adds a **self-introduction builder**, which produces the speech
    you'd give on the first day of class
  - Lesson 2 adds the **flea-market** and **restaurant** role-plays, which
    stand in for Genki's pair work
- **Lesson test.**
  - 12 questions across the lesson; 80% passes
  - a list of what you missed, with the reasons
  - Claude's one-line explanations, when the server key is set
- **Ask Coach** at the bottom of every page sends Claude:
  - the lesson and step you're on
  - what Genki has taught so far
  - the last few questions and answers

  Answers stay inside what Genki has taught so far, flag anything from a
  later lesson, give spoken examples, and say where in Genki to look. It
  runs through `public/grammar.php` (mode `coach`), so the key never reaches
  the browser.

| Game | Drills | Where |
|---|---|---|
| What do you say? | greetings by situation | Greetings |
| Number reader / Number catcher | 0–100 by sight and by ear | Numbers |
| Sentence builder | X は Y です word order | L1 · X は Y です |
| Person or language? | 〜じん vs 〜ご (and why there's no アメリカご) | L1 · X は Y です |
| Which question? / Ages and years | question words; 〜さい and 〜ねんせい sound changes | L1 · か |
| の order | main noun last | L1 · の |
| Clock reader / Phone dictation | hours and halves (よじ, しちじ, くじ); digits by ear | L1 · time |
| Price tags / How much was that? | prices up to ¥99,999 by sight and by ear | L2 · big numbers |
| Point it out / この or これ? / Where is it? | こそあど from both people's side | L2 · こそあど |
| Lost and found | だれの | L2 · だれの |
| は or も? | も replacing は | L2 · も |
| No, it isn't | じゃないです + the right answer | L2 · じゃないです |
| ね or よ? | shared vs new information | L2 · ね/よ |
| Flea market / Lunch order | the whole lesson as a role-play | L2 · shopping |
| Classroom phrases, Lesson N words | matching pairs | warm-up, vocabulary |

The number, time, price and pointing games build each round fresh, so a
second play drills the idea rather than remembered answers. Missed items come
back once at the end of a round. Correct answers earn yennies, as elsewhere
in the app.

## 5. Building the next lessons

Lessons live as data in `apps/web/src/genki-data.ts` (`GenkiLesson`), and
their games live in `apps/web/src/genki-games.ts`. To add a lesson:

1. Write its overview, vocabulary and steps.
2. Write a check and some Ask Coach starter questions for each step.
3. Write an original dialogue and a 12-question test.
4. Add games as needed.

The plan for Lessons 3–12 follows the gaps in section 3. Priorities:

- verb-class and て-form drills with the exceptions mixed in (L3, L6)
- particle-contrast drills (に vs で, L3–4)
- a sorter for time words that do and don't take に
- adjective conjugation, which the book's chart leaves out
- ている with both meanings side by side
- a short-form converter
- solo replacements for every information-gap exercise
